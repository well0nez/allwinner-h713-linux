# Known issues

This is what still gets in your way, and what to do about it. "open" means nobody has fixed it yet;
"by design" means it will not change because something else depends on the current behaviour; "fixed in
git, not in the image" means the source has the fix but the image you can currently install does not yet.

## Not built, or not proven yet

| A 1080p60 source on a 720p panel showed only 43..57 of its 60 frames a second (one repeated, the next skipped) | Fixed 11.10.2026, kernel patch `0255` (`doku/200`). The display firmware downscales 1080p into its three-slot capture ring and its publisher fails there: the flip pointers it rotates for the display name a slot finished one to three frames earlier, hold it every fourth to fifth frame and skip the next, worse over hours - while the capture itself writes every frame in strict rotation. The display driver now takes the slot from the capture's own write index (`INCAP +0xa0c`) and ignores the pointers (`ring_select=0` brings them back): 59.70 of 60 shown over a minute in passthrough, the warp 59.7 fps with none dropped, 1080p50 and 1080p30 each frame once. Ruled out on the way: the MIPS clock, the panel coupling (locks, but its one-line dither flickers and the pixel dither darkens the panel, `doku/199`), the VidDec frame rate, TNR, the bus settings, more ring slots from outside (`doku/197`, `doku/198`). Stock firmware has the same ring and the same pointers. | None needed. The 0.3 frames a second still skipped are what a 59.71 Hz panel cannot show of a 60.00 Hz source. `h713-tv` can offer the panel's own timing first in the EDID (`edid_prefer = panel` in `tv.conf`, off by default since the same evening: 1080p stays 1080p) |

| No HDCP 1.4 | Fixed 05.10.2026. The refusal was real but it was not a wall: the engine declines key-select 3 towards readable memory **by design** (error 0x11 - that would be key export) and accepts it only when the destination is the receiver's key sink **and** the DMA source lies inside the protected window. Both refusals look identical from outside, which is what made this look hopeless for two days, and it is why the eFuse flag reading zero was a red herring - the flag was never what the engine went by. The maker's firmware authenticating on the same unit (04.10.2026) was the clue that mattered. The boot loader now stages the stored key set inside that window and our own EL3 service hands it to the sink; no unwrapping is needed, because the stored bytes are byte-identical to the source of the maker's own engine task. Proven at the picture with a cleared sink as the control, three pairs: `Desired` -> push -> `Enabled` ([`subsystems/hdcp.md`](subsystems/hdcp.md)) | Nothing to do on an image from 05.10. on |
| HDCP 2.2 - one proof outstanding | Proven 05.10.2026: a Fire TV stick on the HY310's input negotiated a 2.2 session (`h713-tv ctl hdcp`: "2.2 (session stands)", authenticated; `doku/152` §2.2, [hdcp](subsystems/hdcp.md)), so the 912 bytes from your device's own secure storage work as they are. Not measured: a 2.2 source after a standby, when `h713-mips-restart` binds the receiver again with the key (`h713-hdcp-key --bind`). *Superseded (05.10.2026):* "what is unverified is whether those 912 bytes are plaintext or a TEE-side ciphertext - that needs an actual HDCP-mandatory source". | none |

On the HY300PRO-G-EU the weak, washed-out picture was the LVDS at 6 bit; U-Boot drives project 0x34 at 8 bit since `2de0cc8`. `test10` carries all of it and follows after `v0.96-beta` ([boards/hy300-pro](../boards/hy300-pro/README.md)) | none needed on the HY310 (its dimmer on PB4 is not wired) |

The media test branch turns the IOMMU on; it is not in `main` yet |
| A damaged H.265 stream can leave the decoder dead until a reboot (media branch) | Measured 13.10.2026 on the HY310 with the media test branch (cstenger's decoder series, IOMMU on): deliberately damaged H.265 clips (one flipped bit, one zeroed 4 KiB block) make the engine read from a wild address (in the two clips traced, while it decoded the picture right after the damaged one, matched by time only). The IOMMU reports it as a page fault (always a read, never a write) and the system stays whole: ssh, the services, `systemctl is-system-running` = running. But after some of these faults every further decode job of every codec times out until `systemctl reboot`; a reset of the engine, a power cycle of its domain and reloading the module do not bring it back. H.264, VP8, VP9 and MPEG-2 survive damaged clips of their own with no fault (VP8, VP9 and MPEG-2 were measured with `0299` v2 loaded, which changes the H.265 path only). Two fixes written from reading the driver (reference-list checks, `0299` v1/v2) measured no effect on the hang and were dropped. What holds the dead state is not known yet; the diagnosis is its own package (`arbeit/medien/mythos/PRUEFUNG-M1-nachtrag2.md`, local). Valid streams are unaffected: 33 clips up to 4K bit-exact against ffmpeg | Reboot after a damaged H.265 file; do not feed streams you do not trust while the decoder's recovery is open |
| Two modes the EDID offers do not lock, and 1080p100/120 are behind a switch | Worked through in working journal `FB-EDID` (`tables/edid-vs-firmware.csv`): every CEA mode block 0 offers has an enabled record in the firmware's table, but two of the base block's standard timings do not - 1600x1200@60 (its records exist and are disabled) and 640x360@85 (no record) - so a PC that picks them gets the console and the line `not in the firmware's table`. 1600x900 is in neither EDID half; a source that sends it chose it on its own, and the answer stays the same line. Block 1 adds 1080p100/120, which the table carries (records 13 and 15), 1080i50/60, and 4K50/60, which it has disabled; `edid = block1` in `tv.conf` (or `h713-tv ctl edid block1`) publishes it as the served EDID through `S_EDID`, without the ARISC's per-port mask. A laptop's 1080p120 locked with block 1 on 02.10.2026; under the keystone warp such a source draws only about 19 frames a second (the GPU is busy, frames are dropped), which is not explained yet | Pick a mode the EDID offers other than 1600x1200 or 640x360; for 1080p100/120 set `edid = block1`, and leave the warp at identity for 120 Hz |

| A frame that arrives while the previous commit is in flight is dropped | `h713-tv` commits the warped frame non-blocking and answers `ok busy` when the previous commit has not flipped yet; the warp then draws the same buffer again instead of queueing. The first run of 22.09. lost 8 to 11 of 1,030 frames; the minute-long A/B of 23.09. lost none. Since 11.10.2026 (evening) the render adds no frame of latency any more: with kernel 0255 the slot handed on is finished, the warp draws it at once (`hold off`); until then it held each slot one vsync, 16.8 ms, because the firmware published a slot before it had finished writing it ([keystone guide](guides/keystone.md)). | Vertical lines on fast motion under the warp | Fixed 23.09.2026, in `v0.95-beta`. The check itself drops frames while it runs | Run the check after any change to the warp, `h713-tv` or the capture driver |
| The settings page has no authentication | Whoever reaches port 8080 can set the picture, move the focus motor and start the autofocus. The Wi-Fi password is the door; on a wired LAN there is none | Change the shipped Wi-Fi password; keep the projector off networks you do not own |
| The automatic keystone corrects against an ideal level pose until it is shown a better one | Without `/etc/h713/keystone-ref` the automatic corrects against the ideal level pose (0, 0, 1 g) and says so on every result line (until 26.09.2026 it waited for the file instead). `h713-keystone reference`, taken with the projector on a surface a spirit level has passed, stores the pose the sensor reads there and prints its offset against the ideal - the sensor's mounting offset, which the stored pose then cancels. A pose more than 45 degrees off flat is refused (the vendor's own gate), and a reference taken under one axis map is refused under another, because its numbers describe a different pose - said once in the journal; the automatic corrects against the ideal meanwhile and the strip says `ref refused` until the reference is taken again. At rest the picture may still breathe by a fraction of a per cent |

## By design

| Behaviour | Reason |
|---|---|
| The warp and the ring are mutually exclusive on the video plane | While `h713-warp` draws, the video plane carries the GPU's NV12 frames with `hdmi-ring` 0 instead of the capture ring - so the display firmware's magnifier (`h713-tv ctl zoom in`), which scales the ring, has nothing to scale and is refused; the nine picture controls act on the warped frame, because it runs through the same channel |
| The GPU userspace is 15 MiB, and panfrost only | mesa is cross-built for this image instead of installed from Debian, where `libgbm1` alone pulls `mesa-libgallium` and `libllvm19` - 187 MiB installed, against a 258 MiB tree. Ours carries the panfrost driver, EGL, GLES2 and GBM and no LLVM, X11, Wayland or glvnd: 15 MiB under `/usr/local`, where a later `apt` mesa cannot collide with it. There is no Vulkan driver, no desktop stack and no second GPU driver in it, and `build-rootfs.sh --mesa none` leaves the whole thing out. |
| No desktop in the shipped image | The image runs `h713-tv`, a fixed HDMI-input service, not a compositor - the projector's job here is the HDMI input. The KMS device (`card1`) is a normal DRM device underneath; nothing stops you from running a compositor on it instead ([docs/subsystems/display.md](subsystems/display.md)). |
| No temperature reading | This unit has no NTC: both of the device's own decompiled device trees say `ntc_num = 0`, and the vendor code treats that as binding rather than fabricating a value (confirmed 2026-09-11, `doku/60` "Der NTC war ein Phantom", patch `0152`). Heat protection runs off the **tachometer** instead - a stalled fan powers the device off (patch `0159`), the same trigger the vendor firmware uses, and the one signal here that is verified (PB5 off -> 0 RPM, on -> 4770-4860 RPM, measured on different days; `board-mgr.md`). A board variant that does carry an NTC picks it up automatically through `ntc_num`. A device that overheats with a *turning* fan is covered by nothing, so keep the vents clear. |
| The projector waits for the power key after being plugged in | Same as with the stock firmware, and deliberate; with crust the remote's power key wakes it too, and it waits at about 0.3 W (measured over FEL on the HY300PRO-G-EU, `doku/191`): [docs/uboot/power-gate.md](uboot/power-gate.md). `fw_setenv h713_gate 0` makes it boot on power instead. |
| `brightness` moves the black level, not the picture | The control writes the picture path's black-level offset (register `0x05001234`, read back following the control on 22.09.2026). On bright content that is invisible, on dark content it shows. It is the vendor's own meaning for this field, and `h713-pq` documents which register each control owns. |
| A source the firmware's table does not carry gets the console | The display firmware's mode table is the list of what locks. A raster outside it is refused and named from the firmware's own record (kernel `0171`), without the "change in flight" of older images: on 02.10.2026 a laptop's 1600x1200 read `1600x1200p60, 162.0 MHz not in the firmware's table`, with one journal line, and the picture came back within two seconds of switching to 1080p60. |
| Setting the EDID block mask publishes nothing by itself | The mask (in-kernel `arisc_hdmi_edid_mask()`, device tree `allwinner,edid-version-mask`, debugfs `edid-version`) stores a byte in the ARISC firmware; the firmware applies it when it next uploads an EDID, at the last fragment. Measured twice on the device, with and without the race that patch `0091e` closes. The default mask 0 is what ships, so port 0 keeps block 0. To make a changed mask visible, trigger a full upload afterwards ([arisc.md](subsystems/arisc.md)). |

## Untested paths

| Path | State |
|---|---|
| Installer on Windows | Not tried for this release; the documentation says so explicitly. `sunxi-fel.exe` for Windows is planned after feedback from Linux users (decision 2026-09-12, `doku/116` section 2). Since layout v4 (2026-09-16) `install` is Linux-only in any case: it mounts the image's own file systems to copy your device's files into them, and says so in one sentence. `identify`, `dump` and `extract` mount nothing. |
| The image's files are copied in through a mount | Layout v4 (2026-09-16, after issue #1): the installer no longer writes the proprietary files into placeholders of a measured size but mounts `hy310-boot` and `hy310-rootfs` and copies them in as ordinary files, so a firmware whose files are bigger than the HY310's fits. An image built for the old layout is refused by this installer with one sentence, and a v4 image likewise by the installer of its own release; use the installer that ships with the image you have. |

## Feature gaps

| Missing | Detail |
|---|---|
| No sounds of its own | Since `v0.96-beta` the DSP mixes the box's own sounds into HDMI (`hw:hy310hdmi,0`, ALSA's default; [audio](subsystems/audio.md)), measured within 0.07 dB of the HDMI level. Nothing in the image plays one yet - no click on the settings page, no confirmation tone. |
| No standby in the release image | The released images switch off into the power gate: 2.9 W, LED red. Suspend to RAM through crust, our ARISC firmware, works on the HY310 in `main` ([standby](subsystems/standby.md)): 2.4 W - less than off -, picture and HDMI sound back about 11 s after the wake, wake by the power key, the remote's IR key or a timer, and a standby that does not finish ends dark in the gate instead of booting with the lamp on (`doku/174`, `doku/179`, `doku/181`). The next release image offers it as an experimental switch (`short = standby` in `/etc/h713/power.conf`, TODO 2.29). Wake by CEC is not supported; the RTC alarm as a wake source is undecided. *Superseded (09.10.2026):* "Off is the power gate: 4 W [...] the power draw in standby is not measured." |
| The remote's keys do nothing yet - **fixed in `main` (11.10.2026), not released yet** | In `main`: [h713-keys](tools/h713-keys.md) gives volume, mute and focus their meaning over Bluetooth and IR, and [h713-osd](tools/h713-osd.md) shows them over the picture; Home, Menu, Back and the arrows are recognised, with no action yet. Over IR the mouse and the microphone key both send `0x66` - by design: they ask for the Bluetooth pairing (`doku/193`). *Superseded 11.10.2026:* "nothing in the image maps them to the picture, the volume or the keystone ... ## The image on your device may not match a fresh build

Everything built before 2026-09-12 20:00 - the development builds of that week, long before the beta
releases - carries a BL31 (TF-A firmware) from 2026-09-10 with debug assertions still compiled in
(49,260 bytes). The build script never rebuilt it because `make` considered the existing binary current;
a clean build produces the intended release BL31 at 45,164 bytes, four bytes of which are just the
embedded build time. `release/build-all.sh` now wipes the TF-A and U-Boot build directories first, so
this is fixed in the source, and the release BL31 has been on the device since 2026-09-15
(STATUS.md "Boot chain"; `doku/116` section 4a P3.9). Only images built before 2026-09-12 still carry the old one.

Details: `doku/61-todo.md`, `doku/60-offen.md`.
